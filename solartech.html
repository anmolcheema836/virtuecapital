The Process

How solar works.

When the sun shines, an abundance of energy is available. Here is the exact journey of that energy into your home.

01. The Sun

When the sun is up, there’s an abundance of free energy available to power your entire home.

02. Solar Panels

PV (photovoltaic) cells in the panels convert this brilliant sunlight directly into DC electricity.

03. Inverter

Converts the DC electricity into usable 240V AC electricity to safely power your appliances.

04. Switchboard

Power enters your switchboard, which seamlessly directs the electricity to different circuits.

05. Battery Storage

Adding a battery stores your excess energy to be used exactly when you need it—like after sunset.

06. The Grid Meter

An import/export meter measures excess energy fed back to the grid, and what you draw when needed.

System Basics

No one-size-fits-all.

01.

Solar Panels Only

Grid-Tied System

PV cells convert sunlight into DC electricity. Your home uses this power first. Any excess energy is seamlessly exported back to the grid. Ideal if you are home during daylight hours.

Most Common
02.

Solar + Battery

Energy Storage

A battery is a great way to maximise the potential of your panels. Utilise the electricity generated when it suits your energy needs, not simply when it is generated.

03.

Off-Grid Systems

Total Independence

Disconnected from the grid entirely. Ideal for remote locations. Requires larger panel arrays, substantial battery storage, and often a backup generator. Much larger investment.

Knowledge Base

Common FAQ's

What happens when it's not sunny? +

In bad weather or winter, solar output reduces 25-50%. At night, panels stop producing. If you have a battery, excess energy stored during the day covers your night usage, effectively giving 24hr solar access.

What happens in a blackout? +

This is where batteries shine. The battery supplies stored energy. Whether it continues charging from panels during an outage depends on having a specific Emergency Power Supply (EPS) setup.

How long does the system last? +

Designed to last over 25 years. We provide 10-year workmanship warranties, 30-year panel warranties, and 10-year battery warranties. Quality solutions lose very little efficiency over time.

Are there government subsidies? +

No, there are currently no government subsidies or incentives for residential solar in New Zealand. The ROI makes sense natively.
