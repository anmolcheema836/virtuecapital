UNIT I: Intro, Demand & Indifference Curves

Q1. Define Managerial Economics. Discuss its Nature, Scope & Relationship with Management. 2025 Exam
Managerial Economics is the "Economics of the Firm". It bridges the gap between abstract economic theory and real-world business decision-making.

1. Definition

Spencer and Siegelman: "The integration of economic theory with business practice for the purpose of facilitating decision making and forward planning."

2. Nature of Managerial Economics

  • Micro-Economic: It studies the problems of an individual firm, not the entire nation.
  • Normative (Prescriptive): It suggests what ought to be done (e.g., "We should lower prices") rather than just describing facts.
  • Pragmatic: It avoids unrealistic assumptions; it deals with practical variables.
  • Uses Macro-Analysis: It analyzes external factors (Tax, Inflation) to make internal decisions.

3. Scope

  • Demand Analysis: Forecasting sales to manage inventory.
  • Production Analysis: Input-Output relationship (Least Cost Combination).
  • Pricing Decisions: Skimming vs Penetration pricing.
  • Capital Management: Investment decisions (Project selection).

4. Relationship with Management

Management is the art of getting things done. Managerial Economics provides the tools (logic, data analysis, optimization) to help management make rational choices under uncertainty.

Q2. Explain Fundamental Concepts: Opportunity Cost, Scarcity, & Incremental Concept.

1. Scarcity

Resources (Land, Labor, Capital) are limited, but human wants are unlimited. This forces managers to make choices. Economics is essentially the study of how to allocate these scarce resources efficiently.

2. Opportunity Cost Principle

  • Definition: The cost of the next best alternative foregone.
  • Example: If a firm uses $1M to buy a machine, the opportunity cost is the interest that money could have earned in a bank.
  • Relevance: Crucial for "Make or Buy" decisions.

3. Incremental Concept

  • Definition: It measures the change in Total Cost or Total Revenue resulting from a specific decision.
  • Rule: A decision is profitable if Incremental Revenue > Incremental Cost.
  • Difference from Marginal: Marginal refers to 1 unit; Incremental refers to a bulk decision (e.g., launching a new product line).
Q3. Explain Demand, Determinants & Price Elasticity. 2025 Exam

1. Demand & Determinants

Demand is the desire backed by ability and willingness to pay.
Determinants (Dx = f...):

  • Price of the good (Px).
  • Income of consumer (Y).
  • Price of related goods (Substitutes/Complements).
  • Tastes and Preferences.

2. Price Elasticity of Demand

The degree of responsiveness of demand to a change in price.

  • Perfectly Inelastic (e=0): Salt (No change).
  • Inelastic (e<1): Necessities.
  • Unitary (e=1): % Change in Demand = % Change in Price.
  • Elastic (e>1): Luxuries.

3. Other Elasticities

  • Income Elasticity: Responsiveness to change in Income. (Positive for normal goods, Negative for Inferior goods).
  • Cross Elasticity: Responsiveness of Good X demand to change in Price of Good Y (Substitutes vs Complements).
Q4. Discuss Demand Forecasting: Significance & Techniques. 2025 Exam
Demand Forecasting is the art of predicting future demand for a product to plan production, finance, and HR.

1. Significance

  • Production Planning: Avoids over-production or under-production.
  • Inventory Control: Reduces storage costs.
  • Pricing Strategy: Helps decide price based on expected demand.

2. Techniques

A. Qualitative Methods (Opinion Based):

  • Expert Opinion Method: Asking industry experts.
  • Delphi Method: A panel of experts answers questionnaires in rounds until consensus is reached.
  • Sales Force Composite: Asking salesmen to estimate sales in their territories.

B. Quantitative Methods (Data Based):

  • Trend Projection: Using past data to project future trends (Time Series).
  • Regression Analysis: Mathematical formula linking Demand to Income/Price.
Q5. Indifference Curve Analysis: Properties & Consumer Equilibrium.

1. Definition

An Indifference Curve (IC) represents combinations of two goods that give the consumer the same level of satisfaction.

2. Properties

  • Downward Sloping: To get more of Good X, you must sacrifice Good Y.
  • Convex to Origin: Due to Diminishing Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS).
  • Non-Intersecting: Two ICs cannot cross.
  • Higher IC = Higher Satisfaction.

3. Consumer Equilibrium (Diagram)

The consumer is in equilibrium where the Budget Line is tangent to the highest possible IC.

Fig 1: Tangency of Budget Line and IC

Condition: Slope of IC (MRS) = Slope of Budget Line (Price Ratio).

UNIT II: Production & Cost

Q6. Production Function: Law of Variable Proportions (Short Run). 2025 Exam
The Production Function shows the relationship between physical inputs and physical output. Q = f(L, K).

Law of Variable Proportions (Short Run)

When one factor (Labor) is variable and others (Land) are fixed, output changes in three stages:

Fig 2: Three Stages of Production
  • Stage 1 (Increasing Returns): TP rises fast, MP rises. Fixed capital is fully utilized.
  • Stage 2 (Diminishing Returns): TP rises slowly, MP falls but is positive. (Rational Producer operates here).
  • Stage 3 (Negative Returns): TP falls, MP is negative. Overcrowding of labor.
Q7. Isoquants & Producer's Equilibrium (Least Cost Combination).

1. Isoquants

An Isoquant represents combinations of Labor and Capital producing the same level of output. It is to production what the Indifference Curve is to consumption.

2. Least Cost Combination

The producer tries to maximize output for a given cost (or minimize cost for a given output).
Equilibrium: Where the Isoquant is tangent to the Isocost Line (Budget Line).

3. Cobb-Douglas Production Function

(Asked in 2025 Short Questions)
Q = A * L^α * K^β. It is a mathematical function showing that output depends on Labor (L) and Capital (K) and their elasticities (α, β).

Q8. Cost Theories: Traditional vs Modern & Determinants. 2025 Exam

1. Determinants of Cost

  • Technology used.
  • Price of inputs (Wages, Rent).
  • Efficiency of management.
  • Scale of production.

2. Traditional vs Modern Theory (Long Run)

  • Traditional Theory (U-Shape): The Long-Run Average Cost (LAC) curve is U-shaped. Costs fall, reach a minimum, and rise due to diseconomies of scale.
  • Modern Theory (L-Shape): The LAC curve is L-shaped.
    • Logic: Modern firms have "Reserve Capacity".
    • Costs fall due to economies of scale and then flatten out (constant). They rarely rise because modern management prevents diseconomies.

3. Explicit vs Implicit Costs

  • Explicit: Out-of-pocket cash expenses (Wages, Rent). Recorded in books.
  • Implicit: Opportunity cost of owned resources (Owner's own salary, Rent on own building). Not recorded in books.

UNIT III: Revenue & Markets

Q9. Revenue Concepts (TR, AR, MR) & Relationship with Elasticity.

1. Concepts

  • TR (Total Revenue): Price × Quantity.
  • AR (Average Revenue): TR / Quantity (This is the Price).
  • MR (Marginal Revenue): Change in TR from selling 1 extra unit.

2. Relationship (Perfect Competition vs Monopoly)

  • Perfect Competition: Price is constant. Therefore, AR = MR = Price (Horizontal Line).
  • Monopoly/Imperfect: Price falls to sell more. AR and MR slope downward. MR falls twice as fast as AR.
Q10. Perfect Competition: Features & Equilibrium. 2025 Exam

1. Features

  • Large number of buyers/sellers.
  • Homogeneous (Identical) Product.
  • Free Entry and Exit.
  • Price Taker: Firm accepts market price.

2. Short Run Equilibrium

Condition: MR = MC. Can have:

  • Supernormal Profit (AR > AC).
  • Normal Profit (AR = AC).
  • Loss (AR < AC).

3. Long Run Equilibrium

Firms only earn Normal Profit.
(If Supernormal profit → New firms enter → Supply ↑ → Price ↓ → Normal Profit).

Q11. Oligopoly: Kinked Demand Curve & Price Leadership. 2025 Exam

1. Oligopoly Features

Few large sellers, Interdependence, High barriers to entry.

2. Kinked Demand Curve (Price Rigidity)

Used to explain why prices stay stable in Oligopoly.

  • Upper Segment (Elastic): If firm raises price, rivals won't follow. Firm loses sales.
  • Lower Segment (Inelastic): If firm lowers price, rivals WILL follow. Firm gains little.
  • Result: A "Kink" at the current price.

3. Price Leadership Model

One dominant firm (Leader) sets the price, and smaller firms (Followers) accept it to avoid price wars.

UNIT IV: Pricing & Factor Pricing

Q12. Pricing Strategies: Skimming, Penetration, etc. 2025 Exam

1. Skimming Pricing

Setting a high price at launch to "skim" the cream of the market (early adopters). E.g., iPhone.

2. Penetration Pricing

Setting a low price at launch to capture market share quickly. E.g., Jio.

3. Cost-Plus Pricing

Cost + Mark-up = Price. (Safest method).

4. Economics of Advertisement Costs

Ads shift the demand curve to the right. The firm spends on ads until Marginal Cost of Ad = Marginal Revenue from extra sales.

Q13. Factor Pricing: Collective Bargaining & Wages. 2025 Exam

Collective Bargaining

Wages are not always determined by supply and demand. In modern markets, they are determined by negotiation between Trade Unions (Labor) and Employers.

  • Upper Limit: Employer's capacity to pay.
  • Lower Limit: Minimum subsistence wage.
  • Outcome: Depends on the bargaining strength of the Union vs the Employer.
Q14. Interest (Real vs Nominal) & Profit Measurement. 2025 Exam

1. Real vs Nominal Interest Rates

  • Nominal Rate: The interest rate stated by the bank (e.g., 7%).
  • Real Rate: Nominal Rate minus Inflation Rate. Represents true purchasing power gained.
  • Equation: Real = Nominal - Inflation.

2. Measurement of Profit

  • Gross Profit: Total Revenue - Explicit Costs.
  • Net Profit (Economic Profit): Total Revenue - (Explicit + Implicit Costs).
  • Note: Economics focuses on Net Profit.

3. Quasi-Rent (Short Note)

Earnings of factors that are fixed in the short run (machines, specialized skill) but variable in the long run.
Formula: TR - Total Variable Cost.
